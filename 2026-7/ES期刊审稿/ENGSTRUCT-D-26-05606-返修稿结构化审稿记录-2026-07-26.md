## 3. 可提交给期刊的英文审稿意见

# Peer-review report

## 1. Manuscript type and scope

- **Article type:** Original research article.
- **Field and design:** Experimental and finite-element investigation of SCF/PLA, CCF/PLA and CCF/Epoxy honeycomb structures under quasi-static axial crushing, including tests with 21700 cells and a height-parametric FE study.
- **Review scope:** Full review of the revised manuscript and the point-by-point responses to four previous reviewers.
- **Limitation of this review:** No raw force–displacement data, FE input files, code or separate supplementary dataset were available.

## 2. Overall assessment

The revision contains substantial new work. In particular, the authors added battery-integrated tests for all three material systems, CT-based observations, material-characterisation curves, mesh-sensitivity results, specimen-level dimensions and a clearer statement of modelling limitations. These additions improve the manuscript.

However, several central issues remain unresolved. Numerical values in the Abstract, Highlights, main text and Table 8 are mutually inconsistent; some reported means and variances cannot be reproduced from the listed specimen values. The experimental cell is described as several different products with different capacities, whereas the FE battery model is based on another cell. Statistical bands are shown without sufficient definitions or sample counts. The battery and integrated FE responses are still described as strongly validated despite a 20% peak-load error and substantial post-peak mismatch. Finally, the proposed height-dependent empirical relations are calibrated and “validated” mainly against outputs of the same FE framework, which is not independent validation. These issues affect the central performance ranking and the claimed predictive design guidance.

## 3. Major comments

1. **Audit and reconcile all central numerical results — Major**
   - **Evidence:** The Abstract reports CCF/PLA SEA values of 18.20 and 24.10 J/g and CCF/Epoxy values of 31.22 and 38.24 J/g (manuscript pp. 1–2), whereas Table 8 and Section 4.2 report 18.48 and 20.07 J/g for CCF/PLA and 43.27 and 38.25 J/g for CCF/Epoxy (pp. 20–21). The Highlights state 18.48 J/g for SCF/PLA, although Table 8 gives 12.88 and 10.32 J/g. The Conclusion states that 38.25 J/g is the highest CCF/Epoxy SEA, while Table 8 contains 43.27 J/g for its 1-cell configuration.
   - **Additional reproducibility check:** From the five SCF/PLA-1Cell peak loads listed in Table 8 (8.02, 7.97, 8.08, 8.10 and 7.98 kN), the mean is 8.03 kN and the population variance is approximately 0.00272 kN², not 8.02 kN and 0.032 as reported. The listed SCF/PLA-8Cell strengths average approximately 7.623 MPa rather than 7.578 MPa. The listed CCF/PLA-8Cell strengths average approximately 18.787 MPa rather than 18.61 MPa, and their population variance is approximately 0.0860 MPa² rather than 0.0018.
   - **Why it matters:** The principal material ranking and several main conclusions rely on these values. The discrepancies cannot be treated as copy-editing errors until the raw curves and calculations are audited.
   - **Required action:** Recalculate every EA, SEA, peak load, compressive strength, mean, SD/variance and CI directly from a single locked dataset. State whether variance is sample or population variance and give units. Replace all inconsistent values in the Abstract, Highlights, text, figures, tables, response letter and Conclusion. Provide the specimen-level raw force–displacement data and calculation script or spreadsheet as research data.

2. **Resolve the identity and properties of the tested and modelled battery cells — Major**
   - **Evidence:** Section 2.1.3 describes a 3.7 V, 4800 mAh cell supplied by Shenzhen Kyoto Electronic Technology (pp. 8–9); Section 2.2 calls it a Panasonic 21700 cell (p. 9); Section 3.1.4 states that the FE model represents a GAIA HP 602030 NCA-45 A/162 Wh cell (p. 16); and Section 4.2 uses a 5000 mAh, 68 g, 18.6 Wh 21700 cell for energy-density calculations (p. 25).
   - **Why it matters:** Cell geometry, internal construction, state of charge, capacity and shell properties strongly affect crush response and short-circuit onset. A literature model for a different cell cannot be assumed to represent the tested cell without calibration.
   - **Required action:** Give one unambiguous manufacturer, model, chemistry, nominal capacity, measured mass, dimensions, lot information and conditioning/SOC protocol for every tested cell. Explain whether the same cell was used in Figures 5, 10 and 12. Either calibrate the FE cell properties against the actual cell or explicitly restrict the model to a phenomenological baseline and remove cell-specific predictive claims. Recalculate the gravimetric energy-density values using the verified cell specification.

3. **Make the statistical analysis reproducible and appropriately cautious — Major**
   - **Evidence:** Section 2.2 states that three repeated tests were conducted for each condition, but SCF/PLA-1Cell has five specimens and the number of battery-only and battery-integrated replicates is not stated. Figures 5, 6 and 10 show “statistical error bars” and “confidence intervals” without defining the confidence level, formula, alignment/interpolation of curves or distinction between the yellow and green bands. Table 8 reports variance but not SD or CI, and several entries are arithmetically inconsistent.
   - **Why it matters:** With mostly n = 3, uncertainty estimates are sensitive to assumptions. Unsupported phrases such as “statistical robustness,” “significantly higher” and definitive material rankings overstate the evidence.
   - **Required action:** Report n for every condition; define the statistical unit, CI level, variance convention and curve-alignment procedure; show specimen-level points for scalar metrics; and report mean with SD or CI consistently. If formal between-material inference is intended, justify and report the model/test and assumptions. Otherwise present the comparisons as descriptive and avoid “significant” language. Explain how the densification point was identified for each curve and whether that determination was blinded or algorithmic.

4. **The FE validation and the height-relation validation are overstated — Major**
   - **Evidence:** For the battery-alone model, the manuscript acknowledges a 20% peak-load error (pp. 27–28). Figure 12(b) shows substantial mismatch in the post-peak response of the battery + SCF/PLA system, despite statements of “strong agreement” and confirmed “reliability.” The height study uses five FE heights (72–80 mm); the empirical relations are calibrated on four configurations and the remaining configuration is called a validation case. Figure 14(c) appears to compare one 72 mm experiment with several numerical curves, not independent experiments at the remaining heights.
   - **Why it matters:** Holding out one output from the same calibrated FE framework tests interpolation of a surrogate, not independent physical predictive validity. It cannot validate the mechanics-based design rule or a general optimum.
   - **Required action:** Separate three levels of evidence: experimental validation of the base FE model, interpolation of FE outputs across height, and external predictive validation. Identify the exact fitting and hold-out heights for each material, report quantitative errors for the entire curve and scalar outputs, and state clearly that the hold-out point is numerical if that is the case. Replace “strong agreement,” “validated FE model” and “optimal” with wording proportional to the evidence unless independent height experiments are added.

5. **Material effects remain confounded with geometry and relative density — Major**
   - **Evidence:** The three systems have substantially different wall thicknesses, specimen dimensions, masses, fibre fractions, void contents and manufacturing architectures. Dividing load by a projected area and EA by specimen mass does not isolate material behaviour from relative density, topology or wall-thickness effects. This was raised directly by Reviewer 4, but the response only restates the definitions of compressive strength and SEA.
   - **Why it matters:** Claims that one “material” provides superior strength or SEA may instead describe the tested material–geometry–process combinations.
   - **Required action:** Report relative density/solid fraction and the precise load-bearing cross-sectional area used for compressive strength. Add an equal-geometry/equal-relative-density comparison if possible. Otherwise consistently frame the ranking as applying only to the tested configurations and discuss the remaining geometry–material confounding as a central limitation.

6. **Manufacturing and material-characterisation details are still insufficient for replication — Major**
   - **Evidence:** The CCF/Epoxy fibre content “can reach approximately 50%” rather than being reported as a measured value. The manuscript does not state CT voxel size, segmentation threshold, analysed volume, number of scans or batch variability. Appendix A shows three material-test curves but does not identify test standards, specimen dimensions, gauge length, loading rate, orientation, conditioning or summary uncertainty. It is also unclear which measured properties, manufacturer data and literature values were ultimately used in each FE model.
   - **Why it matters:** The paper attributes failure and performance differences to impregnation, porosity and interfaces, and uses these properties in FE simulations; those inputs must be traceable.
   - **Required action:** Add the missing manufacturing/CT/test protocols and batch-level statistics. Provide a parameter-provenance table mapping every FE input to “measured in this study,” “manufacturer,” “literature” or “inverse calibrated,” including the calibration target and whether the same data were later used for validation.

7. **Research-data availability and model transparency need to meet the journal requirement — Major reporting issue**
   - **Evidence:** No Data Availability statement or repository link is present in the manuscript. The current *Engineering Structures* Guide for Authors applies its Option C research-data policy: authors are required to deposit and link the supporting data, or state why sharing is not possible.
   - **Why it matters:** Raw curves, thermography/voltage records, CT-derived measurements and FE inputs are necessary to verify the revised statistics and the proposed empirical equations.
   - **Required action:** Add a Data Availability statement and deposit the raw test curves, specimen metadata, analysis code/spreadsheet, FE input decks/material subroutine information where shareable, and the fitting/validation data for Equations (4)–(10). If any item cannot be shared, give a specific reason.

## 4. Minor comments

1. **Figure 5 labelling:** Section 4.1 describes a battery-alone test, but the Figure 5(a) legend reads “CCF/Epoxy-1Cell-Mean.” Correct the legend and clarify the number of cell tests.
2. **Figure 5 caption/axis:** The caption says load, voltage and temperature “versus time,” while the x-axis is displacement. Use one quantity consistently.
3. **Figure 10 panel identity:** Panel (b) is labelled “CCF/Epoxy + Battery” and panel (c) “CCF/PLA + Battery,” whereas the caption assigns (b) to CCF/PLA and (c) to CCF/Epoxy. Reconcile the panel labels, text and caption.
4. **Table 8 scope:** The text says Table 8 contains EA and SEA, but no EA column is shown. Add EA with integration limits or revise the statement.
5. **Response-letter identity:** The response document is headed ENGSTRUCT-D-25-09552, whereas the current manuscript is ENGSTRUCT-D-26-05606. The editor should verify that the response package is correctly associated with this submission and that all quoted revisions correspond to the current file.
6. **Figure 9 citations:** The reference numbering in the Figure 9 caption does not consistently match the preceding text. Audit every structure/reference mapping.
7. **Reference list:** References [22] and [32] appear to duplicate the Uerlich et al. battery-pack study; [47] and [52] appear to duplicate the Wang et al. battery model. Reference [13] concerns concrete columns and does not evidently support the statement about commercial battery-pack materials. Reference [30] concerns laminated windshields and appears unrelated to the adjacent honeycomb/battery discussion. Audit relevance and completeness throughout.
8. **Terminology and typographical errors:** Examples include “Pana,” “Unlich,” “fibre bindle,” “Wide,” “CCF/Eopxy,” “the increase on load,” “with supporting,” and inconsistent CCF/Epoxy capitalization. A further professional language edit is needed.
9. **Equation presentation:** Define every symbol, unit, continuity condition and valid displacement interval in Equations (4)–(10). Explain how the segment boundaries were determined and report fitting metrics and parameter uncertainty.
10. **Article length:** The current body and appendix appear to exceed the journal’s stated 30-page limit for regular research papers (excluding references). Confirm the journal’s page-count convention and condense if required.

## 5. Section-by-section assessment

### Title

The title identifies fibre-reinforced honeycombs and the battery-pack application, but it may imply broader pack-level validation than was performed. Consider specifying “quasi-static axial crushing” and “honeycomb–cell structures” if the current scope is retained.

### Abstract

The topic, materials and combined experimental–numerical approach are clear. The numerical values must be reconciled with Table 8, and the claims of model agreement and design guidance should be qualified. Include the tested loading regime and avoid presenting the height result as broadly optimal.

### Introduction

The application motivation and recent literature coverage have improved. The novelty statement remains mostly qualitative and should distinguish clearly between (i) comparing manufacturing/material configurations, (ii) demonstrating sacrificial load sharing, and (iii) fitting a height-dependent response within a narrow range. Several citations appear irrelevant or duplicated and require audit.

### Methods

The fabrication routes and FE architecture are described more fully than before. Reproducibility is still limited by inconsistent cell identity, incomplete material-test/CT protocols, unclear batch-level fibre fraction, undefined statistical procedures and incomplete provenance of FE parameters.

### Results

The additional CT images, all-material battery tests and uncertainty bands are useful. Nevertheless, central scalar values and statistics are internally inconsistent. Figure labels must be corrected, and the underlying data should be made available.

### Discussion and conclusions

The mechanistic discussion of wall buckling, delamination, fragmentation and frictional dissipation is improved. Some causal statements remain stronger than the evidence, particularly those linking local observations to SEA and those claiming validated design guidance. Conclusions should be restricted to the tested geometries, materials, low-SOC cells and quasi-static axial loading.

### References

The list includes recent work, but duplicated, incomplete and apparently irrelevant references remain. A systematic reference audit is required.

## 6. Reproducibility, reporting and integrity

- **Reproducibility:** Not yet sufficient because raw curves, FE files, analysis scripts and full protocols are unavailable.
- **Statistical reporting:** Not yet reliable; several means/variances fail arithmetic checks and CI construction is undefined.
- **Data/code/material availability:** No manuscript statement or repository link was found.
- **Ethics and safety:** Low-SOC crushing and emergency precautions are now described. The manuscript should additionally identify institutional laboratory risk procedures if required locally; it should not imply that a 129 °C localized response excludes all runaway risk without defining the measurement method and observation window.
- **Integrity concern:** No direct evidence of fabrication or image manipulation was identified from the supplied PDF. However, the contradictory central values and inconsistent versions of Table 8 require a documented data audit before acceptance.

## 7. Language and presentation

The revision is more readable but still contains frequent grammatical and terminological errors, plus figure-caption mismatches. A targeted technical language edit is warranted after the numerical content is locked; editing before the data audit risks propagating further inconsistencies.

## 8. Recommendation

**Major Revision.** The added experiments and analyses are potentially publishable and address several previous concerns, but the paper cannot yet support its central material ranking or predictive-design claims. Acceptance should require a complete numerical audit, unambiguous cell specification, reproducible statistics, more cautious FE validation, explicit limits on geometry/material comparisons, and a compliant research-data statement. If the authors cannot reconcile the raw data with the Abstract, figures and Table 8, the manuscript should not proceed.
